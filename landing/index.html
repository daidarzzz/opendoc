§ 0Preface

Free software under GPL‑3.0. Read it, fork it, make it better.

Offline documentation is a solved problem; the docset format settled that years ago. What was missing was a reader that feels as quick as your editor and as calm as a good book. OpenDoc starts in an instant, keeps every index in memory and stays out of the way, so you can get back to the thing you were actually building.

§ 1Search

Try cpp:vector, or rust,go:channel to search two docsets at once.

Results before you finish typing.

Press Ctrl K anywhere. The palette searches every docset you own through an in‑memory fuzzy index, ranks the results the way you would, and opens the right page with ↵.

<50ms to search across 300,000 entries1
docset:query/ˈdɒk·sɛt ˈkwɪə·ri/

n. A prefix filter. Type a docset’s short name and a colon to search only inside it; separate several with commas. The prefix turns into a chip, so you can keep typing.

cpp:vectorpy:dumpsrust,go:channel

How results are ranked

  1. i.

    Exact match

    dumps → dumps

  2. ii.

    Prefix

    dum → dumps

  3. iii.

    Fuzzy

    jsdmp → json.dumps

  4. iv.

    Last segment

    vector → std::vector

Within each tier, classes and functions step ahead of guides and sections.

1 Matching by nucleo-matcher, the engine behind the Helix editor’s picker. Times depend on your hardware and the size of your library.

2 Zeal’s search rebuilt from its source code (fzy scoring inside SQLite, docsets in parallel) and run beside opendoc-core 0.1.2 on the same machine: Windows, 12 threads, median of three runs. Six queries typed letter by letter over the real CSS, C++, Python 3 and Swift indexes; the larger libraries are copies of them. This times the search engines, not the windows around them. The trade-off: OpenDoc reads every index into memory when it starts, about 0.06 s for these four docsets.

§ 2Reading

Right‑click anywhere for a small, quiet menu instead of the browser’s.

Made for reading, not just looking things up.

Tabs with a memory

Every tab keeps its own back and forward history. Drag to reorder; middle‑click a link to open it right beside the one you’re reading.

Picks up where you left off

Tabs, history and scroll positions survive a restart, written to disk atomically with a backup kept on the side.

No white flashes

Links are prefetched on hover, so the next page is usually ready before you click — and going back stays instant.

Find, zoom, scroll

A real find bar that counts matches, zoom that’s remembered between sessions, eased wheel scrolling and a trackpad that still feels native.

§ 3Library

Already have docsets in a folder? Point OpenDoc at it and you’re done.

Bring a catalog, get a library.

Already have docsets in a folder? Point OpenDoc at it and you’re done.

OpenDoc doesn’t ship docsets or a catalog of its own. Connect a feeds repository you trust — any GitHub repo in the standard feed format — and it becomes a one‑click library: install with live progress, update, uninstall. Slow mirror? It tries another. tarix archives are unpacked without ever touching your originals.

What a connected catalog can hold — a sample installedupdate available

More than 120 language icons ship inside the app, so every entry is recognisable before you’ve downloaded a byte.

§ 4Internals

External links always open in your own browser, never inside the viewer.

Small, quick, and careful with what it opens.

Docsets are HTML from the internet, and OpenDoc treats them that way. Pages render inside a sandboxed frame, served through a dedicated opendoc:// protocol that refuses path traversal, odd encodings and stray symlinks.
